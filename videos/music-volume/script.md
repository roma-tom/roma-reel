## Fact notes

- **"Diners rank noise near the top of their complaints":** Zagat's 2016 national dining survey
  had noise as the second-biggest complaint (25%), behind service (28%), and the top complaint in
  several big cities; its 2018 survey reportedly had noise as the most cited complaint.
  https://www.barandrestaurant.com/operations/your-bar-or-restaurant-noise-polluted ,
  https://www.aarp.org/health/healthy-living/info-2018/noisy-restaurants-hearing-loss/
- **"The average Manhattan restaurant at seventy-eight decibels":** SoundPrint's study of 2,376
  New York City venues (2016 to 2017) reported an average of 78 dBA for Manhattan restaurants,
  presented at an Acoustical Society of America meeting:
  https://acoustics.org/3aaa1-are-restaurants-and-bars-in-new-york-city-too-loud-gregory-scott/
  Said as "one study".
- **"Around seventy or below, people can still talk":** SoundPrint's guidance treats 70 dB and
  below as conducive to conversation (same source); AARP notes normal conversation is about 60 dB
  and at 70 to 80 dB you have to speak up. The gauge zones (good to 70, okay to 76) are drawn
  from that, not a formal standard.
- Phone decibel apps are approximate; "aim for seventy or under" is a practical target.
