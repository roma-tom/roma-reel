## Fact notes

- **"12 YEARS" on Leo's stack:** a cartoon prop for "lots of experience", not a claim.
- **89% attitude, 11% skill; twenty thousand new hires:** Leadership IQ, "Why New Hires Fail" (Mark Murphy, 2005): a three-year study of 20,000 new hires rated by 5,247 hiring managers. 46% failed within 18 months; of those, 89% failed for attitudinal reasons (coachability, emotional intelligence, motivation, temperament) and 11% for lack of technical skill. Also in Murphy's book *Hiring for Attitude* (McGraw-Hill, 2011). Said as "nearly nine in ten who failed".
- **Skills can be taught on the job:** the "hire for attitude, train for skill" idea, long associated with Southwest Airlines (Herb Kelleher) and the Murphy research above. Register and milk steaming are taught on shift at most cafes; said without a time figure.
- **Not either/or:** some roles need experience on day one (a grill cook on a busy line, someone running the kitchen), hence "decide what's truly needed on day one".
- **Paid trial shift:** under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, time spent doing productive work for the business is work time and must be paid, so a working interview or trial shift is paid (U.S. Department of Labor, FLSA hours worked guidance, 29 CFR Part 785).

## Post caption

Lots of experience and a good hire sound like the same thing. Often they're not. The register and steaming milk can be taught on the job. Being warm, reliable and easy to coach is much harder to teach. Leadership IQ followed twenty thousand new hires, and nearly nine in ten of the ones who failed did so over attitude, not skill. Decide which skills you truly need on day one, ask how they'd handle a rush, and try a short, paid trial shift.
