## Fact notes

- **"About an inch of letter height for every ten feet":** a common sign-industry rule of thumb
  for comfortable reading (maximum legibility distance is farther), e.g.
  https://shieldcoart.com/blog/determining-business-sign-size/ and
  https://signazon.com/help-center/sign-letter-height-visibility-chart.aspx . Said as a rule
  sign makers use, not a regulation. Worked example: 20 ft ÷ 10 = 2 inches.
- "They order the one thing they know, or they walk out" is a reasoned claim about behavior,
  not a statistic, and is said without a number.
- The menu prices on the board (5, 4, 9) are illustration only.

## Post caption

If guests can't read your menu board from the door, they don't browse. They order the one thing they know. Sign makers use a simple rule: about an inch of letter height for every ten feet. Stand at the door and read your own board, cut it to fewer items, and put the best sellers biggest.
