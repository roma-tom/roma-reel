## Fact notes

- **Example figures** (said as "Say…"): a cafe with $40,000 in December sales and $24,000 in
  January (a 40% drop), with Sep to Feb shown as an example curve. The January slowdown after
  the holiday season is a widely known pattern for US restaurants; the exact size varies, so
  the video uses an example rather than an industry statistic.
- "Most of it is next month's rent and last month's bills" is framed as the point of the myth
  card, not a measured share.
- The fix is general cash-flow practice (a reserve, paying bills in the month they belong to,
  a January promotion), not a statistic.

## Post caption

December is your best month. It can set up your worst one. Say December brings in $40,000 and January brings in $24,000, but rent, payroll and the December bills all land the same week. Set aside part of December's cash before you touch it, pay December's bills in December, and plan a reason for guests to visit in January.
