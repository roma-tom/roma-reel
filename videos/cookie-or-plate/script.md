# cookie-or-plate

**Kind:** quiz (money)
**Question:** Which earns more: the cheapest thing on your menu or the priciest?
**Background:** paper, pad: sway. **Length:** about 35 s
**Voice:** speech (Kokoro: no ElevenLabs key in this session)

The idea: per sale, the $16 plate keeps far more than the $2 cookie. But the cookie sells all
day, so per day it earns more. Cheap things that sell all day add up.

## Fact notes

All figures are **example numbers for a small cafe**, framed in the voice with "Say…". "Kept"
means the price minus the ingredient (food) cost, before labor, rent and other costs.

- **Cookie:** $2.00 price, $0.40 of ingredients and bag (20% food cost, an example; baked
  goods often run low food cost). Kept: 2.00 − 0.40 = **$1.60**.
- **Plate:** $16.00 price, $5.60 of ingredients (35% food cost, an example in the usual
  restaurant range). Kept: 16.00 − 5.60 = **$10.40**.
- **Per day (example volumes):** 50 cookies × $1.60 = **$80.00**; 6 plates × $10.40 = **$62.40**.
- The point holds whenever cheap items sell enough more often: here the cookie needs to sell
  more than 6.5 times as often as the plate (10.40 ÷ 1.60 = 6.5) to earn more per day.
