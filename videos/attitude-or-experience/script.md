## Fact notes

- **"Almost nine in ten said attitude, not skill":** Leadership IQ's 2005 study of about 20,000
  new hires (as reported by its managers): 46% failed within 18 months, and in 89% of those
  cases the reasons given were attitude (coachability, emotional intelligence, motivation,
  temperament) rather than technical skill. Reported by IndustryWeek,
  https://www.industryweek.com/public-policy/study-nearly-half-new-hires-fail , and in Mark
  Murphy's book "Hiring for Attitude" (https://leadershipiq.com/products/book-hiring-for-attitude).
  It is managers' reports, so the line and the screen say "managers said". The study is older
  (2005) and from a consultancy; the original report wasn't reviewed directly.
- "Steam milk in a week" is an illustration, not a measured training time.
- The fix is common hiring practice (behavioral interview question, watching how candidates
  treat staff, a paid working interview); a trial shift should be paid.

## Post caption

Lots of experience and a good hire sound like the same thing. Often they're not. You can teach someone to steam milk in a week; teaching them to care about a guest takes longer. In one big study, managers said almost nine in ten failed hires came down to attitude, not skill. Ask about their worst shift, watch how they treat everyone, and do a paid trial shift.
