# above-minimum-wage

**Kind:** advice (staff)
**Question:** Should you pay a bit above minimum wage to keep staff?
**Background:** blush, pad: warm. **Length:** about 37 s
**Voice:** speech (Kokoro: no ElevenLabs key in this session)

The idea: losing one barista costs about $2,000 to replace. A $1 an hour raise for a
thirty-hour week costs $1,560 a year. If it keeps her, it pays for itself.

## Fact notes

All money figures are **example numbers for a small independent cafe**, framed in the voice
with "Say your barista Lina quits…". The replacement cost is the same worked example as
`videos/replace-a-barista`, so the channel's numbers agree.

- **Replacing one barista, $2,000 (example):** job ad $150 + interviews $150 (5 hours at $30)
  + training pay $640 (40 hours at $16) + trainer's time $360 (20 hours at $18) + remakes and
  waste $200 + slower line $500 = **$2,000**. Details in `videos/replace-a-barista/script.md`.
- **$1 raise:** $1 × 30 hours a week × 52 weeks = **$1,560 a year** (example hours), less than
  the $2,000 to replace her.
- **Lina's quote** is a character line (a competitor offering a dollar more), not a statistic.
- **Context (not shown):** research on hospitality turnover finds the cost of losing one hourly
  employee runs into the thousands of dollars once hiring, training and lost productivity are
  counted. Tracey, J. B. and Hinkin, T. R., "The Costs of Employee Turnover", Cornell Center for
  Hospitality Research (2006). The video uses its own worked example. The raise only pays off if
  it actually keeps the person, which is why the fix starts with the people you most want to keep.
