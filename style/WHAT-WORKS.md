## What the 13 have in common

**1. The topic decides it, more than the making.** Four topics were made twice, by two sessions,
with different hooks, art and scripts. Both portion creep videos got over 1,600. Both one-star
videos got over 1,300. Both card fee videos stayed under 260, and both "your usual" videos stayed
under 450. Pick the topic well first; polish second.

**2. They're all one of three shapes.** Not one relatable joke, hot take, shout-out,
day-in-the-life, guest-types list or then-and-now video passed 650, out of about 25 made. Advice
and stories did, when they had one of these shapes:

- **A. One item, one price, one surprise.** A single thing a guest buys (a refill, a latte, a
  cookie, a scoop of fries, a $9.95 price tag, one hour of a barista's pay) with a dollar answer
  the viewer wants to guess. Usually two numbers against each other: $2 vs $16, $1 vs $2,000,
  $9.95 vs $10, $14 vs $3.50. Hit rate: 8 of 12. The misses were about an idea, not an item
  (markup vs margin, gift cards, card fees, cash flow) or had no number in the hook (free sample,
  bigger menu).
- **B. A guest is unhappy with you, and you win them back.** The video opens on the guest's own
  complaint, in their words ("Is anyone working?", "My latte was cold. One star."), and ends with
  them as a regular. Hit rate: 3 of 3 with a guest complaint. Stories without a wronged guest
  stayed low (espresso machine 338, Dev's career 373).
- **C. A staff problem with a name or a number.** Something goes wrong for a real-feeling
  person, or costs a clear amount: Ana calls in sick at 6:02, a barista walks out over $1 an hour,
  Dev gets burnt ends while guests get perfect plates, two baristas bonk heads. Process tips with
  no problem in them stayed low (pre-shift huddle, hand off first, menu board, music volume).

**3. The hook opens a loop with a number or a complaint.** Six of the 13 open on a question with
a money answer (free for who? how many lattes? which earns more? $1 or $2,000?). The rest open on
a problem (Ana's sick, one star, nobody's at the counter, same price but not to guests). The low
performers mostly open on a punchline or a statement that closes the loop at once ("2:04 empty.
2:05 bus tour.", "Closed. Almost.", "Post every day? No.", "Ten seconds. Decided.").

**4. Things that don't seem to matter much:** length (winners and losers both run 25 to 38 s),
background color, which template follows the hook, and whether the grid thumbnail is the
designed cover or a frame from the hook.

## Rules that follow

- **Three of every four videos use shape A, B or C.** The fourth can be anything else, to keep the
  feed from feeling like homework, but don't expect it to grow the account.
- **Money videos are about one item.** Name the item in the hook (a refill, a cookie, a scoop, a
  cup, a muffin). Never an abstract idea on its own (margin, cash flow, card fees, gift cards):
  if the topic is abstract, find the single item that carries it ("the $5 latte" for margin).
- **Put a number or a comparison in the hook line**, or a guest's complaint in their own words.
  A question beats a statement. "X or Y?" with two prices is the strongest pattern so far.
- **Stories need a wronged guest or a staff member in trouble,** and a turn where the owner does
  something about it. Warm moments with no problem in them do poorly.
- **Skip for now:** hot takes, shout-outs, day-in-the-life, "every cafe has these guests" lists,
  relatable jokes with no stakes, then-and-now. Bring one back only as the "fourth" video, and
  give it a number or a complaint in the hook anyway.
- **These are early signals, not laws.** About 60 reels, a few hundred to two thousand views
  each, and Instagram's reach is noisy. Re-check when more numbers come in.
